Sim scene: Panda, table, cup, plate

A LIBERO task with a red mug and a plate, plus helpers that move the gripper to an xyz target and open or close it. A scripted rim grasp puts the cup on the plate. Along the way we found that the original push task does not work in sim, and switched to pick-and-place.

The scene

  • Task file sim/cup_on_plate.bddl: LIBERO's living-room table, one red_coffee_mug and one plate, instruction "put the cup on the plate". Success uses LIBERO's own On(mug, plate) check.
  • Start regions in table coordinates: cup at x ∈ [−13, −9] cm (robot side of the table centre), plate at x ∈ [8, 12] cm, both y ∈ [−2, 2] cm, sampled fresh at each reset. Step 1.8 will place them to match the real video.
  • Control: LIBERO's default OSC_POSE delta controller at 20 Hz. An action of 1 on an axis asks for 5 cm of motion that step. The gripper is −1 (open) or +1 (close).

Moving the gripper to a target

move_to(env, obs, target) is a proportional step towards the target. It sends clip((target − eef) / 5 cm, −1, 1) each control step, holds orientation, and stops within 5 mm. On the corners of a 20 × 20 cm square 15 cm above the table:

What didn't work: pushing the cup onto the plate

The plan picked pushing to avoid grasping. A scripted push used the closed gripper as a paddle, moving from behind the cup towards the plate. Over a sweep of push height (1.5, 2.5, 4 cm above the table), speed (full, 0.4×, 0.2×) and push end point, 0 of 21 runs succeeded. Each run failed in one of two ways:

  • The mug tips. At full speed the base catches on the plate rim and the 13.5 cm mug falls over (upright score 0.01–0.2, where 1 is upright).
  • The plate slides. In every run where the mug stayed upright, its height rose 0.0 cm. It never climbed the rim and shoved the plate along the table instead.

Representative rows from the 21-run sweep. "Push end" is where the gripper stops relative to the plate centre. A 13.5 cm mug against a 1.7 cm rim on a light plate is a poor task for pushing, and a real cup and plate would likely behave the same way.

Decision: pick-and-place with a rim grasp

The mug body is 9–10 cm across, wider than the Panda's 8 cm maximum opening, so it can't be grasped around the body. Instead the gripper grasps the rim with one finger inside the mug and one outside. The grasp point is the cup centre plus 4 cm along y, the axis the fingers close on, 3 cm below the top of the rim. The sequence is: hover → descend → close → lift 12 cm → move over the plate → lower → open → retreat.

Consequences for the plan: the task is now "put the cup on the plate", and step 1.7's thumb–index distance → open/close now matters, because the hand's grasp has to trigger the robot's grasp.

Setup notes

  • LIBERO is cloned from source into third_party/ (gitignored, commit 8f1084e). The hf-libero wheel on PyPI ships without the 3D assets.
  • We installed it with --no-deps, because its pinned requirements date from 2022 (torch 1.11-era numpy, transformers 4.21). requirements.txt lists only what the sim needs.
  • This env has its own LIBERO config, set through LIBERO_CONFIG_PATH, so the existing ~/.libero/config.yaml used by another env is left alone. Rendering is headless with MUJOCO_GL=egl.
git clone https://github.com/Lifelong-Robot-Learning/LIBERO.git third_party/LIBERO
conda activate humanoidschallenge
pip install -r requirements.txt
pip install --no-deps -e third_party/LIBERO --config-settings editable_mode=compat
conda env config vars set LIBERO_CONFIG_PATH=$PWD/third_party/libero_config MUJOCO_GL=egl
python sim/scene.py
